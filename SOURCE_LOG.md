# Source log

Accessed 2026-09-23. Methods and data claims use primary sources. Data URLs, download times, sizes, and checksums are recorded in data/download_manifest.csv. Some official documentation PDF endpoints returned HTTP 403 to direct downloads; their content was inspected through web retrieval. Only public-use data were analyzed.

## Official data
- [NHANES data and codebooks](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes/search/datapage.aspx): four cycles, DEMO/PAQ/BMX/SMQ/ALQ/HUQ/DIQ/BPQ/MCQ used. Exact files, sizes and SHA-256 in manifest; cross-cycle variables in dictionary.
- [NCHS weighting tutorial](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes/tutorials/weighting.aspx): use MEC weights and special four-year weights for 1999–2002 when pooling eight years; weights do not fix item nonresponse.
- [NCHS variance tutorial](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes/tutorials/varianceestimation.aspx): masked strata/PSU and domain analysis.
- [Public 2019 mortality directory](https://ftp.cdc.gov/pub/Health_Statistics/NCHS/datalinkage/linked_mortality/): four public .dat files and official R read-in program cached.
- [Mortality data dictionary](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/datalinkage/public-use-linked-mortality-files-data-dictionary.pdf): SEQN, eligibility, vital status, examination follow-up months.
- [2019 linkage methods](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/datalinkage/2019ndi-linkage-methods-and-analytic-considerations-508.pdf), section 4.11; [public file description](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/datalinkage/public-use-linked-mortality-file-description.pdf): some follow-up times and causes are synthetic; final vital status is not perturbed. Ten-year timing is therefore less exact than vital status through 2019.

## Original methodology and software
- Li F, Morgan KL, Zaslavsky AM. *Balancing Covariates via Propensity Score Weighting.* JASA 2018;113:390–400. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2016.1260466); [author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.1785). General balancing weights and the overlap population. The duplicate arXiv 1609.07494 was withdrawn in favor of 1404.1785.
- Zeng Y, Li F, Tong G. *Moving toward best practice when using propensity score weighting in survey observational studies.* [arXiv:2501.16156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16156), v1 (2025) and revised v2 (2026). Methodological preprint on population balancing-weight formulas. Survey weights alone in an individual sandwich are not a substitute for PSU/stratum variance.
- Zhou T, Tong G, Li F, Thomas LE, Li F. *PSweight: An R Package for Propensity Score Weighting Analysis.* R Journal 2022;14(1):282–300. [Article](https://journal.r-project.org/articles/RJ-2022-011/). Both same-named researchers are authors; use exact attribution.
- [PSweight manual](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/PSweight/PSweight.pdf), consulted during the original source review: PSweight_SW accepts survey weights and population estimands, unlike an inference based solely on the 2022 article. It defaults survey.indicator=FALSE and exposes no strata/PSU arguments in the documented interface. Binary fixed-horizon outcomes are feasible if completely ascertained; automatic NHANES design variance is not established. Therefore transparent weights plus survey replicate variance are used, rather than unsupported iid standard errors.
- Lumley T. *Analysis of Complex Survey Samples.* J Stat Softw 2004;9(8). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.18637/jss.v009.i08); survey 4.5. [Replicate design documentation](https://r-survey.r-forge.r-project.org/pkgdown/docs/reference/as.svrepdesign.html). JKn deletes PSUs within strata and allows the first stratum's three PSUs.
- Zubizarreta JR. *Stable Weights that Balance Covariates for Estimation With Incomplete Outcome Data.* JASA 2015;110:910–922. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2015.1023805). Motivates explicit balance/dispersion tradeoffs; SBW is not implemented in this project.
- Hernán MA, Robins JM. *Using Big Data to Emulate a Target Trial When a Randomized Trial Is Not Available.* Am J Epidemiol 2016;183:758–764. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwv254). Trial components guide question specification; this baseline-exposure study is not a full sustained-strategy emulation.

## Directly overlapping clinical literature
- Booker R, Wong M, Boyer W, Gorzelitz J, Carnethon MR, Alexandria SJ. *Associations Between Resistance Training and All-Cause Mortality: NHANES 1999–2006.* First online 2024; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/15598276241248107), [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11562137/). Same cycles and 2019 linkage; derives activity-specific RT sessions/volume and uses Cox analysis. The present PAD440/PAD460 exposure, MEC origin, ten-year marginal risks, selection rules, and estimand comparison differ. The clinical question has therefore already been studied.
- Boyer WR et al. *The independent and combined effects of aerobic physical activity and muscular strengthening activity on all-cause mortality: an analysis of effect modification by race-ethnicity.* J Phys Act Health 2020;17(9):881–888. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1123/jpah.2019-0581); PMID 32732453. Direct same-cycle predecessor; bibliographic identity was checked against the primary bibliographic record. Publisher full-text access failed, so a complete independent full-text appraisal was not performed.
- Zhao G et al. *Leisure-time aerobic physical activity, muscle-strengthening activity and mortality risks among US adults: the NHANES linked mortality study.* [CDC author manuscript](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/151712/cdc_151712_DS1.pdf). Earlier 1999–2004 predecessor. The present analysis is a methodological reanalysis of an existing health question.
